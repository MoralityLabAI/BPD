## Controls and immediate mechanism

The **hard-mixed** negative control had mean 0.97148 gate fraction, mean binary MSE 0.02687, mean read-axis cosine 0.91866, and **zero of 24** phase-routing successes. Dense gates are sensible when both input coordinates are required at every pass; mixed-source reconstruction is a useful guard against universal sparsification.

The **SVD-frozen hard** positive control retained exact axis cosine 1.000 across all 24 seeds, with 15/24 attaining MSE <=.02 and **5/24** achieving the registered >=.95 phase-routing fraction. This makes gate optimization/threshold reliability a concrete limitation even with an exactly aligned dictionary.

The trained hard-disjoint arm recovered axis alignment >=0.90 for **24/24** seeds, with mean cosine 0.99925. Its mean gate fraction of 0.47974 is near the ideal one-of-two fraction 0.5. Most failure is in **false-off component invocations** on one of the active coordinate phases. Examples:
- seed 197: phase 0 gate frequency 1.000/1.000, phase 1 target gate 0.680/0.734, giving just 0.500 of boards complete-phase correct;
- seed 223: phase 0 target frequency 1.000/1.000; phase 1 target 0.820/0.758, giving 0.633 complete-board phase routing.
- seed 137: per-pass target frequencies 1.000, 0.992, 0.969, 0.945; complete-board phase routing 0.906.

Because an exact board-level routing success requires four correct pass masks, small per-pass omission rates compound over the four passes. The result separates **dictionary axis recovery** from **gate reliability**. Hard binary training solved the former under this toy, while the latter drove the preregistered failure.

For stage-two frozen-factor switch selection, 23/24 hard-disjoint seeds chose coefficient 0; seed 179 selected 0.01, reducing its held-out binary MSE from 0.01424 to 0.01247, with its routing fraction increasing 0.8125→0.83594. This is a secondary calibration-selected result and has no bearing on the failed primary stage-one gate. All 96 arms retained exact stage-two dictionary bitwise invariance.

## Boundaries and continuation

- The exact expected-L0 optimal-basis theorem concerns global objective minimizers. Hard 0/1 forward gates with straight-through sigmoid gradients still optimize a differentiable surrogate; they do not supply a global-minimum proof.
- The seed-level paired test evaluates sensitivity to 24 registered random initializations on one 2D synthetic source family. Its quantitative result cannot be transferred to real 7M TRM parameters.
- These results **do not rehabilitate** the failed BPD-v2 Haar rotation null or the full-rank CI gate-identifiability problem. The pinned VPD parity gate and phase-disjoint full-dictionary control remain separate checks.
- The next candidate is a margin-calibrated binary gate or an explicit one-of-two cardinality constraint, plus controls for false-off edits across the full amplitude range. This would be a new prospectively registered model/optimizer experiment; the present failed result is frozen with no retrospective threshold or hyperparameter changes.

**Interpretation:** The model can identify the planted read directions while failing to execute the correct factors on every phase. Successful mechanistic decomposition requires both.
