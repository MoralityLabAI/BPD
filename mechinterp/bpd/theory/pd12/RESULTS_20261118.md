The primary registered test required **at least 40/64** global37 recoveries, ≥24/32 in 8D, ≥12/32 in 16D, ≥16 net paired global37 wins against local37, and exact two-sided McNemar p≤.05. It observed **35/64 overall, 22/32 in 8D, 13/32 in 16D**, and **35 global-only versus zero local-only successes**, giving net +35 and **p=5.820766091346741×10⁻¹¹**.

The global advantage over matched local search is strong under the paired test. The preregistered combined scientific gate **FAILED**, because the overall and 8D absolute recovery floors were missed. The result is retained without adjusting the decision rule or rerunning failed seeds.

Pooled seed-level Wilson 95% intervals: global37 **42.57–66.27%**, global13 **14.75–35.13%**, local37 **0–5.66%**. Global37's separate intervals were **51.43–82.05%** for 8D and **25.52–57.74%** for 16D. Each independent unit is one fixed seed+rotation in one factorial cell. The eight observations within a cell are too few for a decisive cell-level statistical claim.

## Source-specific failure and mechanism interpretation

Uniform strong-margin sources yielded **16/16** global37 recoveries across both dimensions. Unseen k4 support was exact on every test board in those cells. Mean read cosines were approximately 0.9981 (8D) and 0.9957 (16D), with mean physical MSE 0.000920 and 0.001279.

In clustered strong-margin sources, global37 achieved **zero recoveries** at either dimension. Mean read cosines were 0.8615 (8D) and 0.8575 (16D), with minimum matched read cosines near 0.716 and 0.708, consistent with a residual near-45-degree pair mixture. Despite this, mean physical reconstruction MSE was **0.001775** and **0.001687**, respectively. Both cells had **0% exact unseen-quartet four-pass routing**. Low MSE still fails to identify the independently reusable sparse read mechanisms.

In 16D clustered weak-margin sources, global37 obtained mean read cosine 0.8921, minimum cosine 0.6591 and near-zero mean MSE 0.001041. It recovered **zero** complete mechanisms, with mean exact unseen-quartet routing 9.64%. The 8D clustered weak-margin condition recovered 6/8, suggesting interacting effects of dimensionality, source correlation, finite sample volume and the changed σ/gate threshold. This difference is posthoc descriptive and was not a registered interaction hypothesis.

The uniform weak-margin 16D cell recovered 5/8 with global37, while the cheaper global13 arm recovered 0/8. In that cell a tight exact support rule is much more demanding than reconstructing the identity teacher. The global37 16D uniform weak means were cosine 0.9632, minimum matched cosine 0.9029, physical MSE 0.000943 and withheld-quartet routing 61.2%.

## Candidate cost, runtime and budget frontier

The high-budget comparison is exact: 37 angular candidates for each unordered pair, three sweeps, **3,108 candidate pair-angle scores in 8D** and **13,320 in 16D**. The cheaper global13 arm uses **1,092** and **4,680** candidates, respectively, which is 35.14% of the high budget. Each candidate is scored over all **1,024 observed training vectors**, so those are candidate-angle counts rather than total vectorized scalar-score operations. The latter are 3,182,592 and 13,639,680 for global37, compared with 1,118,208 and 4,792,320 for global13.

Measured mean single-run wall time on the same CPU runner:

| Dimension | Global37 | Local37 | Global13 | High/global13 candidate ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8D | 0.2016 s | 0.2059 s | **0.1077 s** | 2.846× |
| 16D | 0.5941 s | 0.6076 s | **0.1913 s** | 2.846× |

Candidate evaluation count scales by **4.286×** from 8D to 16D at fixed angles and sweeps. Measured global37 wall time increased by approximately 2.95× over this particular small CPU setup because fixed overhead and vectorization also affect elapsed time. These are per-arm program runtimes, including sampling and the physical evaluator, rather than isolated model-training wall times. Absolute speed on a different CPU/GPU implementation is unverified.

The reduced-budget arm retained 15/64 full recoveries and was most competitive on **uniform strong-margin** sources, where it succeeded in 15/16 units. It recovered 0/16 weak-margin uniform units and 0/32 clustered units. Its apparent cost advantage comes with source-specific loss of identification accuracy.

## Mathematics, evidence scope and continuation

The latent source is an orthogonally rotated linear sparse activation law, not a nonlinear transformer representation. Every coordinate singleton has positive mass, so the idealized exact expected-L0 read basis remains the rotated coordinate basis up to sign and permutation. The positive-sigma finite-sample Givens optimizer has additional extrema, especially under clustered coactivity and weaker margins.

The registered comparison demonstrates an advantage of global angular reach over equal-budget local search at 8D/16D. It also shows **limits to reliable unsupervised identification** as dimension and support correlation increase, with the strict preregistered 64-unit gate failing. The 13-angle arm provides one point on the measured cost–recovery frontier.

A useful new prospectively registered experiment would vary pair-order heuristics, training sample volume and number of sweeps in **clustered 16D** while retaining the eight-cell first-run results as a fixed reference. Such a test must use new seeds and avoid interpreting 256 observed arms as independent samples. The result does not establish identification of neural mechanisms in the pretrained 7M Sudoku TRM.
