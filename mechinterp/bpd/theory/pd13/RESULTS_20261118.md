# BPD-PD-13: clustered 16D pair-order and training-volume study

**First registered experiment, November 18, 2026. Scientific verdict: FAIL. Engineering verdict: PASS.**

A source-locked 32-seed × 5-arm study tested whether low-zero-mass-first pair ordering and increased training samples repair the failure of nonlocal Givens factor discovery on the original clustered 16-dimensional PD-12 source. The principal treatment and comparator each evaluated exactly 13,320 angular candidates on the same 2048-board training pool. **Both recovered only 2/32 complete mechanisms.**

## Read basis and exact component-support diagnosis

| Arm | Mean matched read cosine | Mean minimum read cosine | Mean held-out MSE | Mean exact unseen quartet routing |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Priority2048 | **0.88680** | 0.70604 | **0.001335** | 6.41% |
| Lex2048 | 0.88131 | 0.69709 | 0.001398 | 6.93% |
| Priority256 | 0.90296 | 0.75341 | 0.001301 | 7.52% |
| Lex256 | 0.86226 | 0.65600 | 0.001506 | 0.10% |

On **clustered strong-margin 16D**, every unlabelled arm recovered **zero of 16** complete mechanisms. The priority2048 read cosine averaged 0.85956 with mean minimum cosine 0.70946, and its reconstruction MSE averaged only 0.0017215. Exact four-pass routing on withheld quartets was **0%**. In **15 of its 16 seeds**, eight matched read cosines exceeded 0.99 while eight were between 0.65 and 0.80, consistent with multiple surviving near-45-degree read mixtures.

On **clustered weak-margin 16D**, priority2048 and lex2048 each recovered 2/16. Their mean aligned read cosines were 0.91404 and 0.90239; withheld-quartet exact routing averaged only 12.83% and 13.87%. Priority256 recovered seed 2579, which both 2048-board arms failed. The sample-size effect is **nonmonotonic at the individual seed level**.

Across all families and 32 seed units, priority2048 made **18,428 required-gate false-offs** and **99,740 redundant false-ons**; lex2048 made **18,714 false-offs** and **114,721 false-ons**. This modest invocation-level improvement did not resolve the factor-identification problem. These counts are descriptive and do not add independent statistical samples.

The strict support metric remains necessary because the shared identity teacher is easy to reconstruct with a mixed orthogonal read basis. Low physical output MSE does not imply correct state-dependent factor selection.

## Matched candidate costs and observed CPU runtime

All four trained arms evaluated **three sweeps × 120 read pairs × 37 angles = 13,320 candidate-angle scores** per seed, using an identical seeded orthogonal initial basis. The two 2048-board arms used the same entire training pool. Both 256-board arms used its exact prefix. Priority pair ordering recomputed current marginal near-zero masses at the start of each sweep, using observed projections only, with no support/count/Q labels.

| Arm | Initial Gaussian near-zero mass | Final mass | Mean nonzero rotations | Mean search time / seed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Priority2048 | 0.279626 | **0.751482** | 173.44 | **3.4096 s** |
| Lex2048 | 0.279626 | 0.738569 | 178.25 | 3.4178 s |
| Priority256 | 0.279624 | 0.760665 | 173.91 | **0.1560 s** |
| Lex256 | 0.279624 | 0.732838 | 177.19 | 0.1552 s |

Priority2048 improved the pooled mean fitted Gaussian objective by **0.012913** versus lex2048. On the strong-margin source, the direction slightly favored lexicographic ordering (0.828752 versus priority 0.828111). The mean objective gain was concentrated in the weak-margin condition, and the registered full mechanism success count was unchanged.

The high-volume arms scored candidates against **8192 observed pass vectors**, resulting in **109,117,440 candidate-by-vector comparisons** per seed; the 256-board arms used **1024 vectors**, giving **13,639,680** comparisons. The priority heuristic separately evaluated **393,216** marginal read/projection pairs per high-volume run or **49,152** per low-volume run. The observed optimizer wall-time ratio was roughly **21.9×** between high and low training volumes on this hosted CPU implementation, despite the 8× nominal scalar comparison count. Wall time is implementation- and hardware-dependent.

## Interpretation and limits

The priority rule slightly improved fitted near-zero mass, mean read-axis cosine and the pooled false-activation census but failed to break the clustered 16D mixed-basis attractor reliably. Increasing training volume eightfold added only a few complete recoveries, all in the weak-margin cell. The planted-Q oracle retained **32/32** successful physical mechanism executions, supporting the validity of the unchanged PD-12 source and evaluator.

Strong and weak source cells differ in active amplitude floor, smoothing width and hard read threshold as frozen in PD-12, so their differences cannot be attributed solely to amplitude. Pair priority itself is compared within matched seeds at each fixed source regime.

Future work needs a separately registered symmetry-breaking intervention on clustered coactivation, with fresh rotations. Candidate hypotheses include randomized pair-order restarts at matched evaluation counts, source-marginal reweighting inferred from observed inputs, or objective terms that distinguish independent latent inactivity from signed coactivation cancellation. The original PD-01 through PD-12 receipts remain unchanged.

The experiment identifies limitations of a finite pairwise optimizer on a designed orthogonally rotated 16D sparse-latent identity teacher. It makes no claim of mechanism recovery in a trained nonlinear recurrent transformer.
