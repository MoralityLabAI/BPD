### Three remaining supervised failures

The supervised-sigmoid failure seeds were **631, 673 and 691**. All three recovered read-axis cosine above 0.96 and achieved perfect full-support routing on overlap and nested trajectories. Their disjoint routing fractions were 74.22%, 8.59%, and 59.38%. Seed 691 reached **100% disjoint count-head accuracy** but still executed the wrong component on some single-support inputs. Directly supervising cardinality does not automatically resolve assignment or factor-coupling errors.

### Why gradient-saturation alone fails as an explanation

Mean first-step count-head gradient norms in the unsupervised arms were approximately **0.155** (original sigmoid), **0.740** (identity-logit), and **0.088** (temperature-2). All three retained **zero of 24** full mechanism recoveries despite those distinct gradient scales.

The post-training fraction of count logits with absolute magnitude at least four was **0.000** for all three unsupervised arms on every held-out family. Their mean signed count margins stayed close to zero, roughly 0.03–0.10, and their disjoint count decisions were near chance.

Under count supervision, sigmoid-STE mean signed margins increased to about **1.77** (disjoint), **2.36** (overlap), and **2.24** (nested). The corresponding first-step gradient norm averaged **0.305**. Count-supervised identity had a larger average first-step gradient (**0.809**) while recovering fewer seeds (14/24). As a **secondary, unadjusted** comparison, supervised sigmoid has seven wins and zero losses against supervised identity, exact paired p=0.015625. It was not the registered primary comparison.

These observations support a failure of the **unsupervised cardinality credit-assignment objective on this finite toy**, with vanishing gradients caused by large count logits disfavored by the recorded evidence. They do not prove a general optimizer convergence theorem or isolate every source of gate error.

## Source-law mathematical interpretation

In this source law, active amplitudes are in [0.75,1.25] and inactive coordinates are exactly zero. The sum of the absolute input coordinates therefore lies in [0.75,1.25] when one component is required and in [1.5,2.5] when two are required. A **fixed linear threshold at 1.375** separates the correct cardinality in the generated task. That simplicity is deliberate: the count-label supervised arm tests a directly learnable auxiliary decision and reveals the training objective's role.

The idealized expected-L0 strict coordinate-basis result from PD-04 still applies to the balanced source mixture, with 1.5 active components/pass and an identifiable read basis. The PD-05 gate is a binary-forward, straight-through differentiable surrogate with a supervised count signal, not an exact expected-L0 optimizer.

The successful result remains restricted to a two-coordinate planted task. A neural BPD claim requires controls with hidden count, support mixing, non-planted read directions, generalization across source distributions, and causal interventions. PD-01 through PD-04 original scientific failures remain unchanged.
