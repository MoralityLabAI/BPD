## Registered population and clean controls

**Eight new independently initialized and jointly trained dense nonlinear four-pass models**, roots 900–907, 640 Adam steps each. Each used 2,048 Gaussian base training examples with **both task markers** and 1,024 separate paired-base discovery / 1,024 paired-base final evaluation cases. Tasks A/B shared the sign(x0) branch for `context>0` and used sign(x1) versus sign(x2) for `context<=0`. External task labels were used only in training and final scoring; projection/discovery and sham admission used the frozen model's own logits and B-task discovery activation geometry.

**All 8/8** passed every preregistered source admission gate: clean accuracy ≥85% for each task and context, dense physical mix, rank-four eigenspace identifiability at all four passes for both tasks, BPD fullmask/weight parity, source-to-B physical write dose alignment, eight context-wise discovery-dose-matched local shams, zero-head negative control and native-versus-orthogonal factor physical/output parity. The lowest root-specific count of qualified shams from 2,048 proposals was **485**, enough for all eight; other roots had 496–534. The source-projector physical gain ranged from **0.9934 to 1.0129**, within its fixed admission range.

The A and B private-branch external target labels disagreed for **48.46%** of paired held-out negative-context cases, demonstrating a real difference between their task contracts. Task-B clean model achieved approximately **95%** aggregate final accuracy. Maximum native/rotated recovered-and-sham complete-rollout logit difference was **4.88758e-6**, below the source-registered 5e-5 threshold. The zero-output-head control returned identically zero score gradients and `UNIDENTIFIABLE` projector in all eight roots.

## Additional predeclared geometry

The rank-four A/B gradient projector **normalized trace overlap**, mean over the four recurrent passes and eight independently trained models, was **0.937675**; roots ranged from **0.872516 to 0.994475**. For independent uniformly random rank-four subspaces in 32D, expected normalized trace overlap is **4/32 = 0.125**. This geometric observation is much stronger than a typical random-basis overlap and helps explain why two different task descriptions can share top gradient directions.

It is **not** a proof of identical computation: a shared final scalar head, shared 32D recurrent transition, common context branch and rank-four gradient projection can all favor overlapping *output-sensitivity* subspaces while leaving task-private information in lower-ranked or differently routed directions. The task-private labels disagree nearly half the time, so a shared top projector does not imply identical behavioral tasks. Root **906** is especially informative: it had the lowest A/B subspace overlap (0.8725) and unusually large **shared** transfer cost (+0.11354), reversing the specificity contrast. These hypotheses are post-hoc interpretation and must be tested on fresh roots under a new source-only protocol.

## Consequences and boundaries

NC-09 supports a reliable *engineering observation* of large A/B rank-four output-gradient subspace overlap inside a single jointly trained model and causal effects on task-B performance under physical interventions. It **fails** the prospective task-specificity and sham-discrimination claims. Its result cannot establish that a shared branch carries an invariant causal module, nor that projectors can transfer between independently trained models with unaligned hidden bases.

A follow-up could pre-register context-conditional gradient covariance, common/private projector subtraction, an independent task-specific readout control, and a causal contrast against source-trained random subspaces on a new root population. The correct science is to retain this original negative study and design an additional experiment around its observed failure mode; **confirmation remains sealed** along with ARG-03-N, AMM-03 and other protected populations.
