## Interpretation and next mechanism

NC-10 demonstrated that **activation charts** across independently trained nonlinear recurrent models are not reliably transportable by ordinary orthogonal alignment. NC-11 adds **local score-Jacobian geometry**, with 128 real B gradient queries budgeted for the aligner and exactly the same number available to the target-only control. Jacobian alignment improves the numerical Procrustes residual and sometimes the causal task NLL relative to activation alignment. However, it loses to the target-only calibrated projector, fails the permuted-pair contrast, and lacks reliable sham specificity. Its original scientific development decision remains **negative**.

This exposes a useful information-theoretic baseline: once target gradients are accessible, directly estimating the target's small causal read subspace from them can be more effective than aligning a high-budget source projector. A future distinct study could test **very low target-query budgets** (e.g. 4/8/16 B gradient rows) and unequal-calibration learning curves, or align explicitly *conditional causal effects under the shared task* rather than only unconditioned output-score sensitivities. Any such work needs a new prospective source and fresh roots; no NC-11 rank, calibration size, sham threshold or metric can be retuned in place.

The eight pairs and Bonferroni root-bootstrap intervals support **synthetic engineering** conclusions only. The 95th-percentile matched-sham tolerance threshold cannot be calibrated with 95% confidence without at least **59 independent sham roots**. Future scientific confirmation remains sealed pending a distinct EvalMath power preflight. Protected ARG-03-N, AMM-03 and all other confirmation populations were never accessed.
