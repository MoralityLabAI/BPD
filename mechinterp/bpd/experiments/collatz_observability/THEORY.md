## 1. The invariant observer is closed

Since `d` is coprime to six, multiplication by two and three permutes the
residues modulo `d`. For every odd core state,

\[
F_d(n)\equiv c(n)2^{-a(n)}n\pmod d.
\]

The multiplier lies in the group generated by two and three. Consequently

\[
q\circ F_d=q.
\]

Thus `q` has an exact autonomous quotient with transition equal to the
identity. Two states with equal `q` continue to have equal `q` at every
horizon. The complete history of this observer repeats the same label.

Task sufficiency has a separate definition. For basin target `Y`, sufficiency
requires a map `b` with `Y=b o q`. At `d=13`, states `1` and `5` share `q`
and belong to different cycles, which disproves this factorization. The
observer's self-closure remains exact.

## 3. Exact future refinement

For any finite deterministic transition `F` and observer `r`, define

\[
n\sim_h m
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r(F^j(n))=r(F^j(m))\quad(0\le j\le h).
\]

Start with the classes of `r`. To form the next partition, group states by
their current class and their successor's current class. If the current
classes encode the word through horizon `h`, the new signature encodes the
word through horizon `h+1`. This proves correctness by induction.

Every strict refinement increases the number of classes. On `N` states,
there are at most `N - |P_0|` strict refinements. Once a refinement leaves
the partition unchanged, equivalent states have equivalent successors.
Induction then gives equality at every later time. The stable partition is
the coarsest equivalence refining the observer and preserved by `F`.

This computation uses future outputs to define an offline equivalence. A
monitor using observations already received has a different information set.

For a uniform odd-core population, the exact optimal basin count for a

\[
\sum_{C\in P}\max_b |\{n\in C:Y(n)=b\}|.

Dividing by the population size gives accuracy. The counting measure is part
of the statement.

## 5. Pure-state distinctions and linear rank

In the specified embedding, the linear observation operator through horizon

\[
\mathcal O_h=\begin{bmatrix}O\\OW\\\vdots\\OW^h\end{bmatrix}.
\]

For `d=13`, exact rational elimination gives ranks `3,4,5,6` at
`h=0,1,2,3`. The rank stays six thereafter. All seven *legal pure states*
already have different observation words at horizon two.

The nonzero vector

\[

\]

satisfies both `O eta=0` and `W eta=0`, which explains an ambient
unobservable direction at all later horizons. It is a linear combination
outside the allowed single-state donor set.

The rank of the stacked linear operator, the number of distinguishable legal
states, an arbitrary finite code's bit length, and a learned BPD factor count
have different definitions. Translating among them requires assumptions about
the state embedding, allowed interventions, precision and decoder cost.

There is also an exact spectral statement in this embedding. A left fixed
vector satisfies `f^T W=f^T`, equivalently `f(F(n))=f(n)` at every state.
Following a trajectory into its eventual cycle shows that `f` is constant
throughout each basin. Conversely, every basin indicator is fixed. Hence
the left fixed-space dimension equals the number of cycles. The indicators
of conserved `q` classes span a subspace of that fixed space because each
`q` class is a union of basins. Across the direct sum of this audit's 172
finite systems, those dimensions are 924 and 653, respectively. The
271-dimensional deficit concerns persistent basin labels in this specified

Any invertible change of basis within that fixed space preserves the

leaves the individual invariant basis vectors unidentified. Basin indicators
have extra structure, including disjoint support and nonnegativity, that can
select a particular representation. This is a finite example of why BPD
factor identifiability requires assumptions beyond conserved behavior.

## 6. Histories with branch and valuation

Suppose the instrument emits a sequence `(c_j,a_j)` of length `h`. Let
`A_0=B_0=u_0=0` and update

\[
B_{j+1}=c_jB_j+2^{A_j},\quad A_{j+1}=A_j+a_j,
\quad u_{j+1}=u_j+\mathbf 1_{c_j=3}.
\]

Composition yields `2^A n_h=3^u n_0+dB`. Since `n_h` is odd,

\[
n_0\equiv(2^A-dB)(3^u)^{-1}\pmod{2^{A+1}}.
\]

Two core seeds with the same emitted word differ by a multiple of
`2^(A+1)`. They coincide if `2^(A+1)>d-1`. Every step has `a_j>=2`,
so `2^(2h+1)>d-1` is sufficient. Length five covers all admitted parameters
through `1025`.

This decoder uses the richer branch-and-valuation channel. The constant
arithmetic observer alone cannot supply its inputs. The decoder also measures
available information; a neural learner's ability to discover and execute it
needs its own experiment.

## 7. Intervention-preserving compression

The stable partition above certifies one autonomous transition. For a
declared intervention alphabet `A` with transitions `F_a`, an encoding `E`
supports intervention-preserving continuation only if

\[
E(n)=E(m)\ \Longrightarrow\ E(F_a(n))=E(F_a(m))
\quad\text{for every }a\in A,
\]

and the required output factors through `E`. Refinement can enforce this by
including the successor class for every allowed `a` in each signature.
BPD masks and global weight edits define additional transitions and must be
included explicitly before that stronger claim is made.

## 8. Containment and cycles

eventual entry into a finite set, followed by eventual periodicity. Nontrivial

while the underlying state moves around one of those cycles.
